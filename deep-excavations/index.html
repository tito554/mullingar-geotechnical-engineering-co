The town sits on Carboniferous limestone overburden with erratic depths to bedrock, often complicated by the proximity of the Royal Canal and the River Brosna. Process overview

Mullingar's population has grown to over 20,000, pushing new commercial and residential developments into tighter urban plots where vertical cuts are unavoidable. A standard cantilever wall simply won't cut it at depths beyond 4.5 metres in the town's stiff glacial till. The till here often contains cobbles and boulders, which can skew standard penetration test results. We model the actual stress-strain response using the Hardening Soil model with small-strain stiffness overlay, calibrated against local triaxial and SPT data. This gives you a realistic picture of ground movement before a single bucket is in the ground. For deep cuts adjacent to masonry structures along Oliver Plunkett Street or near the cathedral, we integrate structural monitoring triggers directly into the design. The output is a complete temporary works package: wall section, embedment, strut levels, and a clear sequence of staged excavation. It's engineering that gets you through planning and keeps the site running.

Local context

Mullingar's underlying limestone is prone to karstic dissolution features—voids and fissures that can collapse during dewatering or drilling. Hit an unmapped swallow hole at 6 metres depth, and your excavation loses material fast, risking sudden settlement at the surface. The glacial till cover masks these features. We always recommend a phased investigation approach: start with a targeted borehole array, then run a cross-hole seismic survey to flag velocity anomalies before finalising the shoring design. Another real risk is basal heave in soft zones where the till transitions into lacustrine clay near the canal. Ignoring the undrained shear strength of these pockets leads to bottom failure, often during the final metre of excavation. We check the factor of safety against heave at every stage, and if it drops below 1.5, we specify a jet-grouted base plug or dewatering with recharge to protect adjacent foundations.

Q&A

What is the typical cost for a deep excavation design in Mullingar?

Design fees for a deep excavation in Mullingar typically range from €1,700 for a straightforward single-level basement shoring check up to €8,730 for a fully modelled multi-strutted excavation with 3D corner effects, groundwater control, and a monitoring plan. The final figure depends on excavation depth, proximity to neighbouring structures, and the complexity of the ground profile. We provide a fixed-price proposal after reviewing your site investigation data.

How deep can you excavate before needing anchored or strutted support?

In Mullingar's glacial till, a cantilever wall can typically manage cuts up to 3.5–4.0 metres, but this depends entirely on the till's undrained shear strength at your specific site. Beyond that, you need a single level of props or ground anchors. We run a limit equilibrium check and a displacement-based model to determine the exact transition point, ensuring the factor of safety never dips below 1.5 for temporary works.

Do I need a separate designer for the temporary works?

Yes, under the Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Construction) Regulations, temporary works design must be carried out by a competent person with specific geotechnical expertise. We act as the dedicated Temporary Works Designer, delivering CAT 3 checked calculations, detailed drawings, and a design basis report that your Project Supervisor for the Design Process (PSDP) can integrate directly into the safety file.
