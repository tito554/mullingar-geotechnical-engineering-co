CPT Cone Penetration Testing in Mullingar – Direct Soil Profiling Without Boreholes

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

The ground beneath Mullingar changes more than most people realise. Over on the west side, near the Royal Canal, we often hit dense limestone till within a couple of metres. Move east towards the Brosna floodplain and you get three to four metres of soft alluvial silts before anything competent. That difference matters when you are sizing foundations. A standard test pit can show you the top layer, but it does not give you the continuous resistance profile needed for settlement analysis. The CPT rig pushes a cone straight down and logs tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure every centimetre. No cuttings, no water, no waiting for lab results. In a town where the water table sits barely a metre below ground in winter, that speed and cleanliness is a genuine advantage. We run the test, you get a digital log the same day, and we sit down to discuss what it means for your bearing capacity and drainage strategy.

A CPT log gives you tip resistance and friction ratio every 10 mm. In Mullingar's mixed glacial soils, that resolution separates a safe bearing stratum from a problem layer.

Process overview

Mullingar sits on Carboniferous limestone bedrock, draped with glacial tills and pockets of glaciolacustrine clay. The real challenge is the variability. One site might be gravel over rock at two metres. The next could have five metres of stiff, fissured clay that looks strong but softens fast when exposed to water. That is where the CPT cone really earns its keep. Friction ratio data helps us separate clean sands from silty clays without a single sample being disturbed. On sites where we suspect loose granular layers, we pair the CPT with a liquefaction screening, using the Robertson method to calculate factor of safety against cyclic softening. This is particularly relevant for any structure over two storeys, given Ireland's low but real seismic hazard under Eurocode 8. We also run dissipation tests to get a direct read on in-situ permeability, which feeds into dewatering design. For road jobs, the continuous profile makes it easy to spot soft spots that a CBR test alone might miss, especially where the pavement will sit on variable fill over peat pockets—a classic Westmeath scenario. The cone does not lie. It gives you a high-resolution picture of exactly what is down there, metre by metre.

Local context

At 100 metres above sea level, Mullingar's topography looks gentle, but the subsurface tells a different story. The last major flood event on the Brosna in 2015 reminded everyone how quickly soft alluvium loses strength under saturated conditions. If you rely on borehole logs alone, you can miss a thin, loose silt layer at four metres that will settle differentially under load. CPT data catches those layers because it records resistance every single centimetre. Skipping this level of detail means risking foundation cracks, floor slab settlement, or retaining wall rotation. On commercial builds near the N4 corridor, we have seen design changes triggered entirely by a CPT profile that showed a weaker zone the borehole log had averaged out. The cone does not average anything. It tells you the truth at every depth increment, and that truth keeps the structure safe over its full design life.

Q&A

How long does a CPT test take on a typical Mullingar site?

A 15-metre profile usually takes 90 minutes to two hours, including setup and breakdown. If we add dissipation tests at two or three depths, expect another 30 to 45 minutes on site. You receive the digital log and preliminary interpretation the same working day.

Can CPT replace boreholes completely?

CPT gives you continuous resistance data but does not recover soil samples. On many Mullingar sites, we combine CPT with a few targeted boreholes or test pits for visual classification and Atterberg limits. The combination gives you both the high-resolution profile and the physical samples needed for a complete ground model.

What does CPT testing cost for a residential project in Mullingar?

For a standard residential site with one CPT sounding to 10–12 metres, expect a budget range of €170 to €210. The exact figure depends on access conditions, number of dissipation tests, and whether we need to pre-drill through fill or hard layers.

What soil types can CPT test effectively in Westmeath?

The cone performs best in clays, silts, sands, and soft to stiff glacial tills—exactly the materials that dominate Mullingar's geology. It cannot penetrate dense gravel, cobbles, or rock. When refusal occurs, we note the depth and recommend complementary methods like rotary drilling to investigate deeper bearing strata.
