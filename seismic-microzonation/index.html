Seismic Microzonation in Mullingar: Site-Specific Ground Response for Safer Structures

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

The undulating drumlin landscape around Mullingar, with its thick veneer of glacial till over Carboniferous limestone, masks a complexity that standard code provisions often miss. In our experience across County Westmeath, the stiffness contrast between compact lodgement till and the softer, water-laden alluvial pockets along the Brosna floodplain can amplify ground motion in ways a generic map never captures. A proper seismic microzonation study resolves this—mapping shear-wave velocity profiles and fundamental site periods block by block. For deeper profiling where bedrock depth is uncertain, we often pair the analysis with seismic refraction to constrain the velocity model before running the full site response.

Mullingar's glacial till can mask a soft infill basin—site period jumps from 0.15 s on till to over 0.6 s on the alluvium, a fourfold difference no regional map shows.

Process overview

Mullingar's growth from a market town on the Royal Canal into a commuter hub for the midlands has pushed new development onto marginal land that was bypassed for centuries. We see housing estates and light industrial units going up on soft lacustrine clays east of town, where the water table sits less than two metres below the surface. The seismic microzonation work here demands a multidisciplinary rhythm: dynamic laboratory testing on undisturbed Shelby tube samples to define modulus reduction curves, combined with field geophysics to nail down the impedance contrasts. A typical campaign in Mullingar delivers five deliverables: a Vs30 map classified per Eurocode 8 ground types, fundamental period contours for the study area, amplification factor maps for short- and long-period spectral ordinates, liquefaction susceptibility zoning where loose saturated silts are present, and a GIS-ready geodatabase that the design engineer can overlay directly on the site plan. Our laboratory runs resonant column and cyclic triaxial tests under ISO 17025 accreditation, ensuring the degradation curves fed into the one-dimensional equivalent-linear analysis are measured, not assumed from generic correlations.

Local context

The Irish midlands experience low but non-negligible seismicity—the 1984 Lleyn Peninsula event (ML 5.4) was felt in Mullingar, and the Irish National Annex to Eurocode 8 assigns a reference peak ground acceleration that, while modest, interacts problematically with soft soil basins. The biggest risk we document in this region is not collapse but serviceability failure: differential settlement triggered by cyclic softening in interbedded silt lenses during a long-duration distant event. A building on shallow footings over a buried channel can rack and crack partition walls while its neighbour on stiff till barely moves. The seismic microzonation explicitly maps these transitions so the structural engineer can place movement joints at the right locations and the geotechnical engineer can specify Improvement—such as stone columns through the soft layer—only where the response spectrum demands it.

Q&A

Does Mullingar really need seismic microzonation given Ireland's low seismicity?

The reference PGA in the Irish National Annex is low, but Eurocode 8 still mandates site-specific studies for ground types S1 and S2, and for importance class III and IV structures. In Mullingar, soft alluvial pockets along the Brosna can classify as S2, triggering the requirement. Beyond code compliance, the cost-benefit of avoiding overconservative Improvement across an entire site by mapping exactly where soft soils exist is compelling for any project over half a hectare.

How long does a microzonation study take from field work to final report?

A typical campaign for a five-hectare site in Mullingar runs six to eight weeks. Field geophysics and drilling take one week; resonant column and cyclic triaxial testing in our ISO 17025 laboratory requires three to four weeks for proper consolidation and staged shearing; and the ground response modelling and GIS deliverable preparation takes an additional one to two weeks. We can compress this timeline for urgent projects by running field and lab work in parallel.

What is the cost range for a seismic microzonation study in Mullingar?

For a parcel between one and five hectares in the Mullingar area, the study cost ranges from €4,030 to €13,310, depending on the number of measurement points, depth to bedrock, and whether dynamic laboratory testing is performed on intact samples or estimated from index properties. Projects requiring non-linear analysis or three-dimensional basin modelling fall at the upper end of that range.

How do you handle the transition zones between stiff till and soft alluvium in the model?

We run separate one-dimensional soil columns on each side of the transition, then apply a lateral interpolation with a smoothing kernel that honours the geophysical section. For critical structures straddling the boundary, we recommend a two-dimensional finite-element site response using a tool like FLAC or Plaxis 2D, which captures basin-edge effects that one-dimensional codes miss.
