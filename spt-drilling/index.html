SPT Testing Mullingar: Reliable Subsurface Data for Construction Projects

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

Mullingar sits at an elevation of roughly 100 metres above sea level, with much of its commercial and residential expansion occurring on glacial till and fluvioglacial deposits left by the last Ice Age. These soils can vary dramatically in density over short distances, which makes a Standard Penetration Test essential before finalising any foundation scheme. The SPT has been the backbone of site investigation in Ireland for decades, providing engineers with a direct measure of soil resistance through the N-value recorded at every 1.5-metre interval. For projects near the Royal Canal or within the town centre where historical fill is common, the test helps identify loose zones that would otherwise go undetected. We run the SPT from a tracked drilling rig that can access tight urban plots and soft rural fields alike, delivering results that feed directly into bearing capacity calculations under IS EN 1997-2:2007. When the investigation requires cross-checking with continuous profiling, our team pairs the SPT with a CPT test to resolve thin layers that the split spoon might miss, particularly in the laminated silts found south of the N4 corridor.

In Mullingar's glacial soils, an SPT N-value above 20 in the boulder clay usually signals bearing capacity exceeding 200 kPa for shallow foundations.

Process overview

A recent project on a greenfield site near Lough Ennell required foundation recommendations for a two-storey nursing home. The ground profile showed loose sandy gravel overlying a stiff boulder clay, and the client needed quick answers to keep the construction programme on track. Using an automatic trip hammer calibrated to the EN ISO 22476-3 standard, we advanced boreholes to 12 metres and recorded N-values every metre through the transition zone. The data showed a clear jump from N=8 in the upper gravel to N=28 once the clay was reached, confirming that a shallow pad foundation could work without deep piling. To verify compaction characteristics of the granular layer, the investigation was supplemented with Proctor tests that established a target dry density for the engineered fill beneath the floor slab. Each SPT run in Mullingar includes careful logging of recovery, colour, and consistency by a geotechnical engineer present on the rig, because the hammer blow count alone never tells the full story when dealing with the calcareous lenses that occasionally appear in Westmeath drift deposits.

Local context

The drilling rig we use across Westmeath is a compact tracked machine fitted with a rotary head and an automatic SPT hammer that delivers a consistent 76-centimetre drop every single time. In Mullingar, the biggest field challenge is not the drilling itself but the water table, which often sits within two metres of the surface along the Brosna floodplain. A shallow water table can cause sand to boil up inside the casing, leading to disturbed samples and artificially low blow counts that misrepresent the true soil strength. Our drillers counteract this by maintaining positive hydrostatic head with drilling fluid and advancing casing ahead of the test zone whenever loose saturated sands are encountered. The split spoon is driven with a measured energy ratio verified by instrumented rod testing, so the N60 value that appears on the final log is corrected for hammer efficiency and overburden pressure. Relying on uncorrected field N-values in Mullingar's variable ground is a shortcut that can push a foundation design toward unnecessary expense or, worse, toward a bearing failure that no one spotted until the cracks appeared in the superstructure.

Q&A

How much does an SPT investigation cost for a single house site in Mullingar?

For a standard residential plot in the Mullingar area, a typical SPT investigation involving two boreholes to 8–10 metres depth falls in the range of €500–€770. The final cost depends on access conditions, number of boreholes, and whether laboratory testing is required on the recovered samples. We provide a fixed-price quote after a brief site visit.

How long does it take to receive the SPT report after fieldwork in Westmeath?

Fieldwork for a typical two-borehole SPT programme in Mullingar is completed in one day. The geotechnical report, including N-value corrections, soil profile descriptions, and foundation recommendations, is usually delivered within five to seven working days. We can expedite the reporting for urgent projects where the contractor is waiting to finalise foundation details.

Is the SPT suitable for the boulder clay found across County Westmeath?

The SPT performs reliably in the stiff boulder clay that underlies much of Mullingar and the surrounding region. When cobbles or boulders are struck, the split spoon may refuse, and the blow count is recorded as a refusal N-value with a note on the log. In these situations, the test still provides useful information by confirming the presence of a very dense stratum, and we often supplement the SPT with rotary coring to examine the lithology of the obstructions.
