The townland of Bellmount on the eastern side of Mullingar and the commercial core around Mount Street share the same postcode but rarely the same ground conditions. One sits on a thin drift veneer over Carboniferous limestone where bedrock refusal can occur within a few metres; the other overlies deeper glaciolacustrine silts where the shear wave velocity profile drops noticeably in the upper 10 metres. In our experience, assuming a single ground type for a development that spans both settings is the fastest route to a costly redesign. The MASW method lets us map the transition from stiff lodgement till to softer basin-fill deposits without putting a single borehole through every boundary, and the resulting Vs30 value anchors the seismic design category directly to Eurocode 8 requirements. For sites where the test pit logs hint at buried peat lenses, we often pair the surface wave data with selective CPT soundings to ground-truth the velocity layering before finalising the ground model.

In Mullingar, the difference between a class B and class C site often hinges on just five metres of intermediate-velocity till that MASW resolves clearly.

Process overview

What we notice repeatedly in Mullingar is that the 30-metre time-averaged shear wave velocity can shift by over 100 m/s across a single site simply because of the way the last glaciation draped drumlinised till over karstified bedrock. The MASW technique uses an active seismic source and a linear array of low-frequency geophones to record surface-wave dispersion, which we then invert to obtain a one-dimensional Vs profile beneath the array centre. A key advantage over crosshole seismic is that no borehole is needed, so the method works well on congested urban lots or on greenfield sites where access for drilling rigs is limited. We typically deploy 24-channel arrays with 2–4.5 Hz geophones and a 10 kg sledgehammer source; the resulting dispersion curve is processed through multiple inversion runs to reduce non-uniqueness. The output feeds directly into the site classification table of I.S. EN 1998-1:2005, placing the ground into categories ranging from A (rock) to D, and occasionally S1 or S2 when deep soft deposits are present.

Local context

A three-storey apartment scheme on the Dublin Road that had been designed assuming ground type B hit a problem when trenching revealed 4 metres of soft lacustrine clay directly beneath the floor slab level. The structural design had to be rechecked because the actual Vs30 placed the site firmly in class D, with a correspondingly higher design ground acceleration. The main risk in Mullingar is not deep basin geometry but rather localised soft infill in glacially scoured depressions, sometimes masked by a stiff desiccated crust that feels firm under a boot. Without shear wave velocity data, these pockets can go undetected by conventional borehole logging alone. MASW provides a continuous velocity cross-section that highlights low-velocity zones, allowing the footing design to accommodate differential stiffness or triggering a ground improvement strategy before construction begins. The consequence of a misclassified site class under Eurocode 8 is an under-designed lateral force-resisting system, which is a liability no certifier in Ireland will accept.

Q&A

What does a MASW survey cost for a typical house site in Mullingar?

For a single-array MASW survey on a residential lot in the Mullingar area, the cost typically ranges from €1,670 to €2,440 plus VAT, depending on array length, number of shot points, and whether passive HVSR recording is added. A firm quotation is provided after reviewing the site location and the intended foundation layout.

Can MASW work on a site with a high water table near the Royal Canal?

Yes, the method is unaffected by groundwater. Surface waves propagate through saturated and unsaturated soils alike, and the inversion process accounts for the slight reduction in S-wave velocity that occurs below the water table. We have successfully acquired clean dispersion curves on canal-side sites in Mullingar where the water table is within a metre of the surface.

How long does a MASW survey take on site?

A single-array MASW line typically takes 60 to 90 minutes to set up, shoot, and dismantle. A multi-line survey covering a half-acre site is usually completed within a single working day. The processing and reporting phase takes three to five working days from the field date.

Is a MASW result accepted by the local authority for planning in County Westmeath?

Yes. Westmeath County Council and consulting engineers in Mullingar routinely accept MASW-derived Vs30 values for Eurocode 8 site classification, provided the report is issued by an ISO 17025 accredited geotechnical laboratory and includes the raw dispersion curves, inversion parameters, and a clear statement of the ground type assigned per I.S. EN 1998-1.
