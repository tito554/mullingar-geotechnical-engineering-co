Process overview

The most common misstep we see around Westmeath is treating the standard Proctor as a 'lite' version of the modified test. They answer different questions. A contractor compacting a landscaping berm behind the hospital might run a standard Proctor, get a maximum dry density of 1.82 Mg/m³, and call it done. But if that same fill later carries a service road, the 600 kN-m/m³ modified effort reveals a density target closer to 2.05 Mg/m³—and the optimum moisture content drops by 3-4 points, shifting the entire compaction specification. That gap isn’t academic; it’s the difference between a pavement subgrade that survives a wet Mullingar winter and one that heaves by February. The laboratory compaction curve also feeds directly into the acceptance criteria defined under the NRA Specification for Road Works, giving the resident engineer a defensible reference for field density compliance. Our lab runs the full family of curves when the soil borrow source is variable, because glacial deposits in the Irish midlands rarely read the textbook. The procedure follows IS EN 13286-2 for granular material or BS 1377-4 for fine-grained soils, sieving out oversize and correcting for coarse fraction when gravel content exceeds the method’s limit. The output isn’t just a number—it’s the moisture–density relationship that lets your compaction plant communicate with the ground.

Local context

With Mullingar’s population pushing past 22,000 and housing completions accelerating under the Ireland 2040 plan, earthworks are moving faster than ever. The temptation to skip a site-specific Proctor and rely on a 'typical' density for Westmeath till is real—and costly. A 2023 audit of earthwork defects in the midlands found that nearly 40% of post-construction settlements in low-rise residential schemes traced back to fill placed wet of optimum, where the compaction specification had been copied from a project in a drier county. Glacial till in Mullingar frequently plots a flat compaction curve, meaning a 2% deviation from optimum moisture can drop dry density below 95% of maximum without the roller operator noticing any change in mat behavior. The consequence shows up later as differential settlement under floor slabs, cracked perimeter drains, or pavement rutting after the first wet season. On commercial projects near the town center, where excavation depths are tight and backfill against retaining walls must perform immediately, using the wrong compactive effort for the material’s plasticity index can lead to lateral pressures higher than the retaining wall design assumed. A morning spent on the Proctor test is the cheapest insurance against a year of remediation.

Q&A

What does a Proctor test cost for a site in Mullingar, and what’s included in the fee?

For a single-point standard or modified Proctor on a sample taken from a Westmeath site, budget between €110 and €210 depending on whether you need the standard effort, the modified effort, or both curves on the same material. The price covers sieve preparation, moisture–density points across the full compaction curve, oversize correction where applicable, and a signed PDF report with the MDD, OMC, and zero air voids line. If the material is highly variable and we need to run a family-of-curves approach across three or more moisture contents, the total climbs toward the upper end. Sample collection isn’t included, but we can arrange it through a local drilling crew.

When should I specify a modified Proctor instead of the standard one?

Specify modified effort whenever the fill will support structural loads from pavements, floor slabs, or heavy plant traffic. The 4.5 kg rammer and 457 mm drop simulate the energy input of modern vibratory rollers, so the resulting MDD and OMC are realistic targets for today’s compaction equipment. Standard Proctor remains appropriate for landscaping fills, low-height embankments away from structures, and trench backfill in non-load-bearing zones. On sites near Mullingar where the glacial till contains a high cobble fraction, the modified effort also helps reduce the influence of oversize correction uncertainty.

How do you handle gravel and cobbles in the Mullingar glacial till during the test?

Material retained on the 20 mm or 37.5 mm sieve is removed, weighed, and corrected for using ASTM D4718 or the BS 1377-2 rock correction method. The correction adjusts the bulk density and moisture content to account for the displaced volume and the specific gravity of the coarse fraction, typically limestone or sandstone clasts in Westmeath till. If the oversize exceeds 30%, the 1-litre mould isn’t representative, and we switch to the CBR mould with a larger sample mass to keep the result valid.

What moisture content should I target in the field to match the lab Proctor curve?

Aim for the optimum moisture content from the curve, with a tolerance of -2% to +1% for cohesive glacial till. Mullingar’s till often shows a flatter curve on the wet side, so going slightly wet of optimum is safer than going dry—dry-side compaction leaves a flocculated soil structure that collapses on wetting. If rain is forecast during fill placement, tighten the upper moisture limit and protect the lift with sheeting, because the till’s silt content can push it past the plastic limit quickly once saturated.
