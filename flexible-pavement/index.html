Flexible Pavement Design in Mullingar: Geotechnical Inputs for Long-Life Roads

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

When we bring a heavy nuclear density gauge and a dynamic cone penetrometer into a site on the outskirts of Mullingar, we are preparing the ground for a pavement that must withstand decades of traffic loading on glacial till. The flexible pavement design process begins with careful subgrade evaluation because the limestone-derived tills around Mullingar can vary from stiff boulder clay to softer, moisture-sensitive silts within a few hundred metres. We extract Shelby tube samples for resilient modulus testing and run in-situ CBR tests using a DCP rig calibrated to I.S. EN ISO 22476-2. Before any layer thickness calculation begins, we also look at drainage conditions, because the drumlin landscape that defines much of Westmeath creates localised perched water tables that can weaken the subgrade seasonally. For projects where the pavement will carry heavy HGV loads, the subgrade modulus is cross-checked with in-situ permeability results to design an effective capillary break layer.

A subgrade CBR increase from 2% to 6% can reduce the required asphalt thickness by nearly 40%, making early geotechnical investigation the most cost-effective step in any Mullingar pavement project.

Process overview

The glacial till that underlies the Mullingar area, with its mix of cohesive matrix and subangular clasts, poses a specific challenge for pavement designers: the material can exhibit high stiffness at low moisture but loses strength rapidly when saturated. Our team addresses this by running a sequence of laboratory tests that go well beyond basic Proctor compaction. We determine the optimum moisture content and maximum dry density per BS 1377-4, but we also measure the California Bearing Ratio at multiple saturation levels to capture worst-case spring-thaw conditions. The resilient modulus is derived from repeated load triaxial tests following EN 13286-7, which gives us a reliable input for the mechanistic-empirical design method outlined in the NRA DMRB. Where the subgrade CBR falls below 2%, we evaluate stabilisation options using lime or cement, verifying the improved modulus with grain-size analysis to confirm that the binder will react effectively with the fines fraction. The pavement structure is then optimised layer by layer, balancing asphalt thickness, granular base, and capping layer to achieve a 40-year design life for major routes.

Local context

The wet Westmeath climate, with annual rainfall exceeding 1,000 mm, makes moisture control the dominant risk factor for flexible pavements across Mullingar. A pavement designed on the assumption of a drained subgrade can fail prematurely if the groundwater level rises within the capping layer during prolonged wet winters, leading to pore pressure build-up and loss of bearing capacity. We see this mechanism regularly on secondary roads where the original construction omitted adequate side drains. To mitigate the risk, our designs incorporate a granular capping layer with a minimum 600 mm thickness above the formation, combined with filter geotextiles that prevent fines migration. We also model the effect of freeze-thaw cycles, which can cause ice lens formation in silty tills and subsequent softening during the thaw. By specifying a pavement foundation that remains above the predicted frost penetration depth and using non-frost-susceptible granular materials, we ensure that the structural integrity holds through the coldest Mullingar winters.

Q&A

How much does a flexible pavement design for a private road or car park in Mullingar typically cost?

For a typical project in the Mullingar area, the full design package — including field CBR testing, laboratory resilient modulus determination, and the NRA-compliant layer thickness report — falls in the range of €1,570 to €5,170, depending on the pavement area and the number of test locations required. A small car park with four test pits will sit at the lower end, while a longer access road with extensive subgrade variability pushes toward the upper end.

What CBR value do you design for on glacial till subgrades around Mullingar?

We design for the soaked CBR value, not the dry-weather value, because the drumlin tills around Mullingar can lose more than half their strength when saturated. Our field programme measures CBR at multiple depths using a DCP calibrated per I.S. EN ISO 22476-2, and we also run laboratory CBR on remoulded samples at the target density and optimum moisture content. The design CBR is then selected as the lower quartile of all measured values to account for spatial variability.

Do you handle the pavement design submission for Westmeath County Council planning?

Yes. The design report we produce follows the NRA DMRB structure and includes all the supporting geotechnical data, traffic classification, and layer justification that the Council requires. We have delivered compliant submissions for developments along the N4 corridor and for industrial estates around Mullingar Business Park, and we coordinate directly with the planning consultant to ensure the pavement section drawings meet the conditions attached to the grant of permission.
