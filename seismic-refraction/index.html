Seismic Tomography Surveys in Mullingar: Refraction and Reflection for Site Characterisation

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

The ground beneath Mullingar tells two very different stories. Over by the Brosna floodplain near the town centre, you get soft alluvial silts and peats that dampen seismic signals fast. Head south toward Rochfortbridge and you hit limestone bedrock barely three metres down, with hard reflectors that bounce energy cleanly. Seismic tomography bridges that gap. We use both refraction and reflection methods to image subsurface layers under the Royal Canal corridor and beyond, where glacial tills and karstified limestone create complex velocity contrasts. Mullingar sits at roughly 100 metres elevation on the Irish midlands, and its carboniferous limestone underlies everything we do. Getting the velocity model right here means factoring in water table depth, which fluctuates seasonally and affects P-wave transmission through the upper weathered zone. Our field crew has worked seismic lines from the N4 bypass route to infill sites near Columb Barracks, adapting spread geometry to urban constraints without losing resolution.

Karst cavities in limestone can collapse a velocity tomogram if you don't cross-check with reflection events. We map both the overburden and the voids.

Process overview

A practical observation from years of shooting seismic lines across Westmeath: the drift cover in Mullingar rarely exceeds 12 metres before you hit rockhead, but the transition zone is deceptive. Weathered limestone can look like competent rock on a refraction tomogram if you don't run a reflection overlay to confirm the true bedrock reflector. Our acquisition uses 24-channel geophone spreads with a sledgehammer source for shallow targets, stepping up to accelerated weight-drop when penetration beyond 30 metres is needed. The P-wave refraction data gives us a continuous velocity gradient, which we convert to rippability ratings for excavation planning. For sites where shear modulus matters, such as footing design on variable ground, we combine the tomography line with MASW to extract Vs profiles down to 30 metres. Every shot gather is checked in the field for trigger timing errors and bad traces, because re-shooting a line in Mullingar after mobilisation costs time and client budget.

Local context

The limestone around Mullingar isn't solid everywhere. The midlands have a history of karst development, and in areas like Lynn or toward Lough Ennell, we've mapped dissolution features that create sudden velocity drops in the tomogram. A bedrock surface that looks continuous on a single refraction profile can hide a clay-filled cavity two metres wide. That's why reflection tomography matters here: the acoustic impedance contrast between air/water-filled voids and competent limestone generates clear reflectors that refraction alone won't image. The other risk is seasonal saturation. When the water table rises in winter, P-wave velocities in the upper till can jump by 300 m/s, masking weak zones. Our team runs paired dry-season and wet-season checks on long-duration projects to avoid misinterpreting saturation effects as lithology changes.

Q&A

What is the difference between refraction and reflection seismic tomography?

Refraction tomography maps the velocity of compressional waves that travel along the top of a higher-velocity layer, such as bedrock. It works best where velocity increases with depth. Reflection tomography records energy bounced back from acoustic impedance contrasts, so it images layer boundaries directly, regardless of velocity structure. In Mullingar's limestone terrain, we often run both because karst cavities can break the refraction assumption of increasing velocity, while reflection will show the void as a clear reflector.

How much does a seismic tomography survey cost for a typical Mullingar site?

For a standard residential or small commercial lot in Mullingar, expect between €2,290 and €4,320 depending on line length, number of spreads, and whether reflection is added to the refraction survey. Urban sites with restricted access or night-time noise constraints fall toward the upper end of that range.

How deep can seismic tomography see in the Mullingar area?

With a sledgehammer source and 24-channel spread, we typically reach 25 to 30 metres in competent limestone. Using an accelerated weight-drop, penetration can extend to 40 metres or more. Depth is limited by energy attenuation in the glacial till cover, which varies across town: thicker drift near the Brosna reduces penetration compared to the shallow rock sites south of Mullingar.

Can seismic tomography detect sinkholes or cavities in limestone?

Yes, but the method matters. Refraction alone may miss a clay-filled cavity because the velocity contrast is small. Reflection profiling is far more sensitive to the acoustic impedance change at a cavity boundary. On karst-prone sites in Mullingar, we recommend a combined refraction-reflection acquisition to map both the general bedrock topography and any anomalous voids.
