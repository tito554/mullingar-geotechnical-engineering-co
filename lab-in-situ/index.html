In-Situ in Mullingar

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

In-Situ in Mullingar provides direct measurement of ground conditions without disturbing the natural fabric of soils and weak rock beneath the site. The underlying geology here is dominated by Dinantian limestones of the Lucan Formation, often mantled by glacial tills and alluvial deposits along the Brosna River corridor. These variable drift materials—ranging from stiff boulder clays to soft silts in floodplain zones—demand site-specific data that only field tests can deliver. Our investigation services integrate these tests from the earliest phase, ensuring compliance with Irish Standard I.S. EN 1997-2 (Eurocode 7: Ground investigation and testing) and the current Building Regulations Technical Guidance Document B. Where cohesive layers or potential karst features are suspected, we pair in-situ techniques with targeted In-Situ protocols to capture a complete geotechnical profile.

Methodology follows the rigorous framework set out in the Irish National Annex to Eurocode 7 and the relevant IS EN ISO standards. The electric piezocone Cone Penetration Test is a primary tool for soft to firm deposits, logging continuous tip resistance, sleeve friction, and pore pressure to derive undrained shear strength and stratigraphic boundaries without a borehole. For engineered fill and pavement layers, the field density test (sand cone method) is performed to IS EN 13286-2, verifying compaction against specified relative density or air voids criteria. When cohesive soils are encountered, disturbed samples are taken for laboratory index testing, including Atterberg limits, which define the liquid and plastic limits critical for classifying fine-grained soils under the Irish SR 21:2014+A1:2016 guidance. All field instruments are calibrated daily against NIST-traceable references, and penetration tests are halted at refusal on dense till or limestone bedrock.

Typical projects in Mullingar reflect the town’s growing residential and light industrial footprint. We regularly deploy In-Situ for single-house builds on greenfield sites along the C-Link road corridors, where glacial till thickness can vary from 1.5 to over 6 metres, requiring CPT soundings to confirm bearing capacity for foundations. Road widening schemes and retail park developments on the N52 approach rely on sand cone density tests to sign off on Type 1 sub-base compaction before asphalt paving. In the town centre, where historical flooding influenced alluvial silt deposition, our combination of CPT pore pressure dissipation tests and laboratory grain size analysis (sieve + hydrometer) provides the drainage parameters needed for soakaway design to BRE Digest 365. Every project references the laboratory validation of field data to ensure consistency between in-situ behaviour and classified index properties.

The process begins with a desk study of Geological Survey Ireland mapping and a site walkover to select test locations. Field crews then execute the agreed scope, with CPT rigs logging at 2 cm intervals and density tests performed at formation level or within each compacted lift. Data is transmitted daily; we deliver preliminary plots within 24 hours and a final interpretative report within five working days. The deliverable includes tabulated test results, corrected CPT parameters, stratigraphic logs, and direct geotechnical design parameters such as constrained modulus and undrained shear strength, formatted for immediate use by structural engineers. Clients gain a single, defensible data set that reduces reliance on conservative presumptive values, cutting foundation concrete volumes and eliminating over-excavation risk—a practical, code-compliant solution for Mullingar’s ground conditions.

Q&A

What's the difference between active and passive anchors?

An active anchor is stressed to a design lock-off load before the structure takes full load—it actively restrains movement. A passive anchor only develops resistance once the ground or structure starts to move; it relies on tendon elongation under load. In practice, active anchors suit stiff retaining walls where you want to limit deflection, while passive anchors work for soil nailing or rock bolts where some displacement is acceptable. The choice affects the bonded length calculation and the testing regime under IS EN 1997-1.

How much does anchor design and testing cost in Mullingar?

Design-only fees typically range from €980 for a straightforward temporary tie-back to around €3,410 for a permanent anchor system with full corrosion protection, detailed drawings, and on-site proof testing coordination. The spread depends on the number of anchors, the complexity of the ground profile, and whether cyclic or creep testing is specified by the supervising engineer.

What testing is required after installation?

We follow the testing schedule in IS EN 1997-1 and the execution standard IS EN 1537. Every anchor undergoes acceptance testing to 1.25 times the design load. A subset—typically 5% or at least three anchors—goes to proof load testing at 1.5 times the design load. For permanent anchors in Mullingar's variable ground, we often recommend creep tests at 1.35 times the working load, held for 15 to 60 minutes depending on substrate type.
