Laboratory CBR Testing in Mullingar: Reliable Subgrade Strength Assessment

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

Around Mullingar, the ground can be deceptive. You might start an access road project near Lough Ennell and find perfectly competent gravel, then move half a kilometre and hit a pocket of laminated clay that softens the moment it sees water. We have seen this pattern across dozens of sites—from the new housing estates off the C-Link road to industrial units near the N4. The laboratory CBR test cuts through that variability and gives you a number you can use directly in pavement thickness design. When we combine it with a grain size analysis on the same bulk sample, we can usually explain why a soil is behaving the way it does, which is half the battle in Westmeath's glacial till country. Our soaked CBR procedure follows BS 1377-4:1990 and the TII (Transport Infrastructure Ireland) specification, so the result plugs straight into Irish pavement design methods.

A soaked CBR test tells you what the soil does when it's at its weakest—not when it's dry and flattering you with a high number.

Process overview

We recently worked on a commercial yard in the Marlinstown area where the contractor had brought in crushed limestone fill from a local quarry near Rochfortbridge. The material looked solid in the dry, but the spec required a soaked CBR of at least 30 percent for the capping layer. Our lab compacted three specimens at different moisture contents—optimum, optimum plus two percent, and optimum minus two percent—then submerged them for 96 hours under a 4.5 kg surcharge. The Proctor compaction tests we ran first gave us the moisture-density relationship, which is essential because the CBR value can drop by half if you are just two percent wet of optimum. For projects where we need to check the in-place density afterwards, we pair the lab CBR with sand cone density testing to verify that the field compaction matches the laboratory benchmark. The full test sequence takes about five working days from sample receipt to report, including the soaking period and penetration readings at standard intervals.

Local context

The CBR plunger assembly in our lab is a 50 kN load frame with a calibrated proving ring and a 49.6 mm diameter penetration piston. We run it in a temperature-controlled room at 20 ± 2 degrees Celsius because the penetration resistance of cohesive soils is temperature-sensitive—a detail that gets overlooked in field labs. Skipping the soaked condition is the most common shortcut we see, and it produces misleadingly optimistic numbers. Mullingar's water table is high across much of the townland, with winter groundwater often within a metre of the surface. A soil that gives CBR 25 unsoaked can collapse to CBR 4 after four days of saturation, which means a pavement designed on the dry value will rut within two winters. For road schemes requiring subgrade improvement, the soaked CBR result tells us whether we need to specify stone columns or can achieve the required stiffness with lime stabilisation and re-compaction alone.

Q&A

How much does a laboratory CBR test cost in Mullingar?

A single-point soaked CBR test with the associated Proctor compaction curve typically falls between €120 and €210, depending on whether we test one, three, or five compaction points and how much sample preparation is required. A three-point CBR with full moisture-density relationship and swell monitoring is at the upper end of that range.

How long does it take to get CBR results from your lab?

The minimum turnaround is five working days. The soaking period alone takes 96 hours as specified by BS 1377-4, and we need the preceding day for compaction and setup. If we receive material on a Monday, the final report is typically ready by the following Monday. Rush processing is possible for an additional fee if the lab schedule allows.

Do you need disturbed or undisturbed samples for the laboratory CBR test?

Disturbed bulk samples are the standard—we need about 25 kg of material passing the 20 mm sieve, sealed in a heavy-duty bag to preserve the natural moisture content. If the soil contains particles larger than 20 mm, we sieve those out, record the percentage, and test the finer fraction, applying a correction factor in the report. Undisturbed sampling is not required for CBR because we recompact the material in the lab mould.

What CBR value is acceptable for a road subgrade in County Westmeath?

TII specifications require a soaked CBR of at least 15 percent for a subgrade to support a standard flexible pavement without stabilisation. Values between 5 and 15 percent typically require capping layers or lime treatment. Below CBR 5, the subgrade is considered very weak, and we generally recommend either a thicker capping layer, geogrid reinforcement, or Improvement measures before pavement construction begins.
