Vibrocompaction Design in Mullingar: Improvement for Glacial Soils

Practical geotechnics, field-tested.

LEARN MORE

In Mullingar, many sites sit on a blanket of loose, saturated glacial till over limestone bedrock. What we see repeatedly is that standard compaction methods cannot reach the depths required to densify these deposits, especially where the water table is high, just a metre or two below ground level. A well-planned vibrocompaction design becomes the critical path to a stable foundation, mitigating differential settlement before it ever becomes a problem. The approach relies on depth-specific vibratory probes that rearrange grain structure through cyclic horizontal forces, and we routinely combine this with a CPT test before and after treatment to verify the improvement in cone resistance across the full profile, ensuring the design assumptions hold true in the ground.

In glacial sands, achieving 70% relative density through vibrocompaction can reduce post-construction settlement by an order of magnitude, but only if the probe grid is matched to the local grain size curve.

Process overview

The soils beneath Mullingar are dominated by the legacy of the last glaciation: a mix of boulder clays, glaciofluvial sands, and occasional pockets of peat. The clean, poorly graded sands of the Esker Riada, which cuts across the county, can be particularly susceptible to densification under dynamic loading. Our vibrocompaction designs are calibrated to this glacial environment, specifying probe spacing, vibration frequency, and dwell time based on grain-size distribution data from grain-size analysis. The typical target is a relative density above 70%, measured through post-treatment CPT and cross-hole seismic tests. We also evaluate the risk of fines migration into the limestone fissures that underlie much of the town centre, a local hydrogeological nuance that generic designs often miss. For sites where the till is too silty for pure vibrocompaction, the design may integrate stone columns as a complementary drainage and densification technique, particularly along the Brosna River floodplain where saturation levels are persistent.

Local context

Under Eurocode 7, the design of vibrocompaction in Mullingar must account for the Limitation State GEO for bearing capacity and STR for structural settlement. The real risk is not just total settlement but differential settlement across a transition zone—where treated ground meets untreated ground, or where the thickness of loose glacial deposits changes abruptly over a few metres laterally. This is common near the drumlin edges around Lough Owel. In our experience, ignoring the pre-treatment variability of the till leads to underestimating the post-treatment performance. A thorough site investigation, correlating CPT tip resistance with SPT N-values through SPT drilling logs, is the only way to build a reliable ground model. Without it, the design grid may leave untreated lenses that later consolidate unevenly, causing cracking in stiff masonry structures common in the town.

Q&A

What types of soil in Mullingar are suitable for vibrocompaction?

The technique works best on clean sands and gravelly sands with a fines content below about 12%. Much of Mullingar's glaciofluvial material, especially along the Esker Riada corridor, falls into this category. If the glacial till is too silty or contains too much clay, we will typically advise a different Improvement method, such as stone columns, because the fines prevent the necessary grain rearrangement during vibration.

How much does a vibrocompaction design and treatment programme cost for a typical Mullingar site?

For a standard residential or light commercial site in the Mullingar area, the full service—including the feasibility study, design, trial section, and production treatment—generally ranges from €1.360 to €4.070, depending on the treatment depth, grid density, and the extent of the verification testing required. A site-specific quote is always provided after the initial ground investigation data is reviewed.

How is the performance of vibrocompaction verified after treatment?

Verification relies on pre- and post-treatment cone penetration tests (CPT) carried out at the same location to measure the increase in cone resistance. We also look at the reduction in ground surface settlement around the probe during treatment. In critical structures, cross-hole seismic tests may be added to confirm the increase in shear wave velocity, which correlates directly with improved stiffness and density.

What depth of improvement can vibrocompaction achieve in the glacial deposits around Lough Owel?

In the sands and gravels near Lough Owel, we can typically treat depths from 5 metres down to about 25 metres with conventional hanging-lead vibrators. The limiting factor is usually the depth to bedrock or the presence of very dense boulder layers. The maximum effective depth is assessed during the design phase using the CPT refusal depths from the site investigation.
